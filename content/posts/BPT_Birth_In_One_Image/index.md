That's not a joke about a forever confused CEO. It's the default state of most AI hopefuls.

## AI Demo Slides Induced Perpetual State of Confusion

Every "AI" vendor pitch, every all-hands, every roadmap slide is full of words everyone nods at: "agentic," "AI-native," "transformation." Nobody stops the meeting to ask what they actually mean for *this* business, *this* process, *this* P&L line.

So the nodding continues. Budgets get approved. (AI) Pilot gets funded. And six months later, nobody can explain why the thing doesn't work — because nobody could explain, at the start, what "working" was supposed to look like.

That's not a model problem. It's a comprehension problem.

## The Gap Isn't Between "AI" and "No AI"

It's between people who can say what a process actually does and people who are nodding along because the words sound right. 

**The Operational Model Was Always Missing, AI Speed Just Made it Obvious**

The [BPT](https://method.dbj.org/bpt/bpt_operating_model.html) OP model, part of the [DBJ Method](https://method.dbj.org/), doesn't add more vocabulary to nod at. If you can't say it in BPT, you don't understand it. You're not just nodding any more. 

AI doesn't expose who has the best operational model. AI exposes who actually knows what that means. 

Get the right side of the image right first, in your organization. It is not a one-week intro, but do deploy the [BPT Operational Model](https://method.dbj.org/bpt), with the whole crew holding the [CMM L3](https://method.dbj.org/cmm/dbj_cmm.html#levels) pass card as the entry precondition. L5 is the target. The horizon will widen dramatically.

Funny as it seems, that right side of this image is the true picture of three roles enjoying the BPT OP model, having coffee in the pause of a confusing presentation.
