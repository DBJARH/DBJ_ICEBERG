The [BPT loop](https://method.dbj.org/bpt/) is not free-for-all, first comes first served. Different roles are assigned to different segments. That fact defines **jurisdiction** — the boundary within which a role operates, decides, and is accountable.

Jurisdiction is not hierarchy. A Business Leader is not senior to an Engineer in the BPT loop. They operate in different domain with different role bound concerns. (hint: EA is the only role whose jurisdiction is the whole organisation).

---

## Enterprise Architecture jurisdiction

EA's jurisdiction is the whole organisation — not a segment, not a project. This is what makes it the governing role.

EA does not implement, does not manage products, and does not operate infrastructure. It governs the structure within which all of those happen.

---

## Why this works

- Organisations at sufficient [CMM level](https://method.dbj.org/cmm/) have implicit governance structures — roles respect boundaries because the organisation is mature enough to enforce them
- Product management becomes the **operational manifestation** of architecture — the alignment point is staffed and accountable
- The loop tests architecture decisions against reality continuously — Evaluate is not optional activity
- The question *"Is Business aligned with Technology?"* becomes measurable rather than political slogan

---

## Domain naming and role naming

The BPT domain are named **Business**, **Product**, and **Technology**. The roles inside the Technology segment are primarily Engineers and DevOps — *Engineering* describes the roles, *Technology* is the domain name. This distinction matters when reading diagrams and deliverables: the domain name is always Technology.

Definitions and terms may appear in singular or plural depending on the size of the organisation. It is rare for there to be only one product or one team. The model scales — see [One wheel or many](https://method.dbj.org/adm/multi_wheel.html#one-or-many).
